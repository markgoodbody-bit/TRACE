## Scope of verdict

This means Chapter 7 has a viable book voice and successfully opens Part III by shifting from diagnosis to witness, record, and enforcement absence.

It does not mean final, canon, validation, proof, release, or external readiness.

## Why it passes

### 1. It changes the energy of the book

The opening is severe and direct:

```text
At some point the person understands that no one is coming.

Not today. Not in time. Maybe not at all.
```

This is the right tonal shift for Part III. The book stops analysing access and timing and begins dealing with enforcement absence.

### 2. The existence/arrival distinction works

```text
But existence is not arrival.
```

This is a strong continuation of the book's false-door logic. A regulator, court, charity, union, or appeal body can exist without arriving in the person's world in time.

### 3. It avoids record-as-justice confusion

The chapter does not romanticise documentation:

```text
A record is not justice.

A record does not remove mould, return sleep, pay rent, stop retaliation, reopen an account, reverse a public accusation, or make someone with power care.
```

This is necessary. It prevents the witness turn from becoming fake comfort.

### 4. The main line lands

```text
A record is not justice, but disappearance is worse.
```

This should remain one of the book's central sentences.

### 5. Witness is introduced without heroics

The chapter explicitly refuses heroism, rescue, martyrdom, publicity, and grand performance. This is correct.

```text
Witness begins when someone refuses to let a harm become invisible just because enforcement is absent.
```

This is the chapter's core definition.

### 6. The discipline section is essential

The section beginning "Do not record everything publicly" is one of the most important safeguards in the chapter.

It prevents the book from accidentally encouraging unsafe exposure, spectacle, or evidence-hoarding.

### 7. The ending points correctly to custody

```text
The next question is where the record can live without being lost, captured, or turned against the person who made it.

That question is custody.
```

This is a clean bridge to Chapter 8.

## Remaining small issues

These do not block the verdict.

### 1. The chapter is slightly list-heavy

The first third includes several lists: possible authorities, possible routes, possible evidence actions, possible absent enforcement actions. They work because Part III needs severity and breadth. Later edit may trim one list if the rhythm feels crowded.

### 2. The file/record pattern should now be used sparingly

The chapter uses "The file says / The record says" effectively, but the book has used this rhythm many times. Chapter 8 should vary the device unless needed.

### 3. Witness needs custody immediately

The chapter correctly says witness needs discipline, but Chapter 8 must now carry that discipline into custody. Otherwise witness risks sounding abstract.

## Book structure implication

Part III has begun:

```trace
Part_III :=
  witness_under_enforcement_absence
  + custody
  + residue_burden
```

Chapter 7 establishes that witness is not justice, not proof, not rescue, and not publicity. It is preservation under enforcement absence.
