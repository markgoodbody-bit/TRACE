## Scope of verdict

This means Chapter 8 has a viable book voice and successfully carries the witness turn into custody without becoming a witness-pack manual.

It does not mean final, canon, validation, proof, release, or external readiness.

## Why it passes

### 1. It starts with the right question

```text
Where can the record live?
```

This is the correct opening. It turns custody into a lived question rather than a technical storage problem.

### 2. It makes custody concrete

The early list works because it names actual homes for records:

```text
drawer, phone, cloud folder, email thread, notebook, solicitor's file, union inbox, charity case note, support worker's archive, journalist's encrypted folder, neighbour's photograph, public post, private message, printed timeline
```

This grounds the chapter immediately.

### 3. The custody/storage distinction is strong

```text
Custody is not storage.

Storage asks: where is the file?

Custody asks: what happens to the person because the file is there?
```

This is the chapter's central hinge. Keep it.

### 4. The main claim lands

```text
A record only matters if it can survive without becoming another risk to the person who made it.
```

This should remain the chapter's load-bearing sentence.

### 5. It avoids manual voice

The chapter names practical risks without turning into a checklist or operational guide. The prose remains book-like even when handling metadata, holders, refusal, context, and decay.

### 6. The holder section works

The contrast between good and bad holders is useful:

```text
A good holder can say no.
```

and:

```text
A bad holder receives everything and calls that help.
```

This is precise. It makes refusal part of care.

### 7. The anti-extraction passage is necessary

```text
Without custody, witness can become another extraction.
```

This is one of the most important safeguards in Part III. It prevents witness from becoming story-taking.

### 8. The decay/context distinction works

```text
A record without context can survive as an object and die as evidence.
```

This is strong and should remain.

### 9. It bridges cleanly to Chapter 9

The ending points to residue and burden:

```text
Because even when a record survives, the person may still be the one carrying the debt.
```

This is the correct bridge.

## Remaining small issues

These do not block the verdict.

### 1. One typo/minor line edit needed later

The line:

```text
Links expire. phones break.
```

should later be corrected to:

```text
Links expire. Phones break.
```

Do not patch as a separate file now unless doing a line-edit pass.

### 2. The chapter is dense but controlled

It carries many practical risks: metadata, exposure, capture, refusal, custody, extraction, decay, context. It remains readable, but later book edit may break one long section into shorter breath units.

### 3. Chapter 9 should become more human again

Chapter 8 necessarily handles practical custody. Chapter 9 should return strongly to the person left carrying the cost. It should be less infrastructural and more morally direct.
