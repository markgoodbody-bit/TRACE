# TRACE v0.4 beta3 — intellectual neighbours

Status: **BETA SUPPORT / ATTRIBUTION MAP / NOT A CLAIM OF OWNERSHIP OR CONFORMANCE**.

TRACE is an integration language. The existence of a stronger or older owner is a reason to learn from it, not to hide overlap. This file records nearby structures that informed or pressure the v0.4 beta. It does not claim those works endorse TRACE, that TRACE conforms to them, or that the list is exhaustive.

## Viability / reachable continuation

- Jean-Pierre Aubin, *Viability Theory*; later Aubin, Bayen & Saint-Pierre, *Viability Theory: New Directions*. Viability kernels are the clear mathematical neighbour of TRACE's survival/viability-set language. TRACE keeps designation, measure, affected scope and evidence authorship visible rather than treating the constraint set as a discovered moral fact.
- Amartya Sen's capability approach, especially the distinction between resources and substantive opportunities people can actually convert into valued doings and beings. This is a strong neighbour for `ROUTE_EXISTS != ROUTE_USABLE` and for keeping circumstances/conversion constraints visible.

## Future-space / conservative agency

- Victoria Krakovna et al., “Penalizing side effects using stepwise relative reachability” (2018). Relative reachability makes baseline choice explicit and demonstrates why naive preservation of reversibility/options can create bad incentives.
- Alexander Matt Turner, Dylan Hadfield-Menell & Prasad Tadepalli, “Conservative Agency via Attainable Utility Preservation” (2019/2020). AUP is a strong neighbour for preserving future attainable capability under uncertain objectives.

TRACE does **not** import either work as a moral score. The v0.4 beta uses them as pressure to expose comparison baselines and to avoid `MORE_OPTIONS = BETTER`.

## Supported prospect / hope

- C. R. Snyder et al., “The Will and the Ways: Development and Validation of an Individual-Differences Measure of Hope” (1991). Snyder's hope theory separates agency and pathways around goals; it is a major psychological neighbour of the route/actor structure in TRACE's supported-prospect view.

TRACE keeps the represented route and actor conditions while dropping the psychological “sense of” construct. It does not infer felt hope, define a hope measure, or claim Snyder conformance.

## Timing / systems safety / scheduling

- Nancy Leveson's STAMP / systems-theoretic safety work treats safety as a control problem in complex sociotechnical systems, with control structures and feedback across levels. TRACE should interoperate with this machinery where causal control and feedback are the real problem rather than recreating safety engineering.
- Critical-path, PERT/CPM, real-time scheduling and formal timing methods are stronger owners for detailed precedence, resource and stochastic-feasibility questions. TRACE beta3 therefore moves detailed window machinery out of the compact spine and keeps only the evidence/boundary discipline needed to know when a strong timing claim is earned.

## Answerability / sociotechnical challenge routes

- Kekulluoglu et al., *Answerability by design in sociotechnical systems* (2023), explicitly studies how people affected by autonomous decisions can ask questions, challenge decisions and seek remedies. This is a direct neighbour for TRACE's answer-back / correction-route concerns.

TRACE does not claim conformance with STAMP, scheduling theory or answerability-by-design research. Their existence is a reason to hand off technical machinery and keep TRACE focused on cross-domain discrimination.

## Formation / longitudinal change

- John Dewey, *Human Nature and Conduct* (1922), especially habit as interaction between organism and environment and the idea that conduct helps form later dispositions.

TRACE deliberately keeps the derived formation view thinner: supported changes in capability, relation, constraint, burden or future-space may be represented without inferring a total character or inner essence.

## Exit / voice / cooperative governance

- Albert O. Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970), for the distinction between leaving an arrangement and changing it from within.
- Elinor Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990), for governance arrangements in which affected participants, boundaries, monitoring, conflict resolution and nested responsibility matter.

These are neighbouring owners for refusal, exit, voice and cooperative maintenance. TRACE does not convert their domain-specific findings into universal laws.
