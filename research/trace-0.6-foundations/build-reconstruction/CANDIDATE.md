# TRACE 0.6

**How can we make a better future?** Mark originated TRACE as a navigation language for an unfamiliar AI. A human can read and challenge the same account. You can use it from the position of someone choosing, someone affected or someone trying to understand what happened.

Use the [short spine](SPINE.md) for a first decision. The [mathematical companion](README.md) gives the inputs, calculations and executable examples. The [workshop encounter](#7-paired-worked-probe) follows what the people involved learn, make possible and leave unfinished. The [guide to earlier sources](EDITION-SOURCES.md) takes you to the exact definitions, examples and optional contracts this edition draws on.

## Start with the next decision

1. **Give the account a job.** Name what someone needs to understand, choose or make possible now, whose purposes might differ, and the period that matters. Let the inquiry change the question. If you are using Mechanical Ethics, state which commitments guide this use, such as making harm visible, keeping correction reachable or building useful capability without hiding its costs.
2. **Follow what could change it.** Keep the source, status and scope of evidence the next step depends on. When something seems to have failed, ask what observation could distinguish the plausible causes before assigning a repair. For a choice on a clock, work back from the effect to the latest useful answer. If a cutoff or the time needed to act is unknown, say when you plan to check and what timing you still need. Carry unresolved questions that could change the choice.
3. **Find a usable next move.** Check who can take it, with what agreement, authority, time and resources. To keep an alternative within reach, check what its holder has agreed to keep available, for whom, until when and at whose cost. Ask while their answer can still change the plan. For protection, follow the move through to its effect before the particular loss hardens.
4. **Return what someone can use.** Carry what actually happened, any new ability or changed purpose, unresolved costs and what would require a fresh decision. End with a next act someone can take up. Distinguish a question still to ask, a request already sent and the answer its holder actually gave.

In the [bicycle workshop](CANDIDATE.md#what-next-saturday-needs), A came to learn repairs and C needs a quiet place to take part. After several sessions, the next organiser gets this handover.

> The organiser saw A mend a puncture on the practice wheel without help. A has a kit at home. How A manages both brakes there is still unknown. Last Saturday’s borrowed tools were returned. A new session needs a keeper to release a kit, a willing volunteer to collect and return it, and someone to open a quiet room C can use. A and C each need time to decide whether to come while other plans are still possible.

Ask A what they would like to do next. If they still want to adjust their own brakes, offer to look for a session where A can do the work and a qualified helper can check it. Ask separately whether A wants to teach puncture repair and whether their name can go on the notice. Ask C directly, while another plan is still possible, whether they want to come.

Suppose A chooses to practise privately and asks the organiser to leave their name off the public teaching list. Close that listing. A has made a choice about publicity and can still take the practice place. Record who chose the limit and what it covers. If a possible teaching slot turns up next month, first check who can open the room and provide the kit. Once those arrangements hold, A can decide whether to teach. Anyone counting on a second session needs to know who has agreed to keep it available and for how long.

### One navigation pass

The workshop organiser counts attendance. A says they want to try the brake adjustment unaided and have a qualified person check it before they leave. Keep the attendance record and add the question A’s account makes relevant. What can A do and have checked within the time they have?

The organiser can offer one slot. A 20-minute demonstration lets A watch. A 40-minute practice lets A attempt the adjustment and have it checked. The room and kit are ready, both offers are authorised, and a qualified helper has agreed to check this bike today. For this example, give the 20- and 40-minute windows equal chances and assume A takes an offer that fits. If all the organiser knows is that either window is possible, the odds remain open. A still decides what to do in the room, including whether to stop or leave.

| Outcome by A’s departure limit | Demonstration | Practice | Practice minus demonstration |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A attends (the offer fits and A takes it) | 1 | 1/2 | −1/2 |
| A tries unaided and the helper checks the attempt | 0 | 1/2 | +1/2 |

Use the same evidence and deadline for each pair of figures. Practice is the offer that can meet A’s stated aim in this example. Before A leaves, ask what A tried and what the helper actually checked. Return the result to A while they can still correct the account and decide what to do with it. Before promising another session, [confirm the helper’s agreement for that session and any venue payment](CANDIDATE.md#carry-the-helpers-agreement-and-the-cost). A’s ability to do the job at home remains open.

Can the venue and helper keep the slot changeable long enough for A to choose how to use it? If they agree, keep the confirmed demonstration available and [ask while A’s reply can change the booking](CANDIDATE.md#let-as-reply-change-the-booking).

Use the [full working and glyph key](CANDIDATE.md#one-offer-two-outcomes) to see where evidence, choice and observable change enter the calculation. The [booking example](CANDIDATE.md#as-departure-and-the-next-offer) uses a strict reservation lock and an inclusive departure limit. Use ordinary words or a specialist method whenever they carry the answer more economically. Stop adding detail when it cannot affect this decision. Reopen when the evidence, support or purpose changes.

## 1. Begin with a living situation

Ask what is happening and what could be formed, enjoyed, maintained, repaired or lost. For a consequential change, say what you are comparing, for whom, over what period and from whose view. An entity, group or institution sees only part of a changing world. The world, signals, accounts, maps and retained records are different. Treat the boundary and the list of participants as provisional. Ask whose options come into view if you draw the boundary differently. Moral standing remains a separate question. Other entities have perspectives and possibilities of their own. Keep what a source asserts separate from its warrant, our access to it and anyone's decision. An assertion can have a definite value while its evidence is unavailable or disputed. A missing answer is different again; silence alone establishes neither withholding nor refusal. Lack of a recorded route is not proof that no route exists. [Which table did Sam’s helper check?](CANDIDATE.md#which-table-did-sams-helper-check)

For each consequential claim name its source, scope, time, evidence and relevant competing account. A recorded “yes” can have unknown support. A reported “no” is a negative claim with an identifiable source. An empty entry leaves even the source’s answer open. In the [original notation](../../../TRACE-v0.5.md#2-a-compact-typed-review-notation), the asserted value and its evidential status have separate fields. Separate a report from the event reported, the status warranted under a named contract from the truth of its whole contents, and a corrected record from a changed world or repaired harm. Witnesses may need protected custody; a useful record must not force unsafe disclosure. Send, receipt, understanding and effective downstream use differ.

Keep the time a claim is about separate from when a report is received. A point observation does not by itself establish that a condition continues. Observation is through a named aperture, not a view of the whole world; a report does not become an observation merely by passing a format check. A source identifier or hash of a local request does not by itself authenticate who supplied a response or establish a witness's independence.

Different participants can hold conflicting maps, aims and clocks. Some processes have no evidenced selector; in others selection can be distributed. Inquiry can itself change the situation being observed. A working-belief map is held, reported or attributed from a stated aperture; it is not direct access to hidden mental or model states. A stored counteraccount is not by itself a change of belief, and a change of belief does not by itself improve an outcome.

Not asked, inaccessible, disputed and deliberately withheld can overlap; where the difference matters, retain the holder, scope and source. A person's choice to withhold is a boundary, not automatically a gap others are entitled to fill. Silence alone proves neither refusal nor withholding.

When uncertainty is an input to action or delay, identify the actor, policy or default that selects it where the evidence permits; uncertainty itself is not a selector. The responsible selector may remain unknown or distributed.

Sam has given standing authorisation to use a table made from documents. A new version has arrived and the helper has gone home. In Mark Goodbody’s case, the handover needs two records. The first names who compared which version with its originals. The second records what Sam decided to do with that version.

| What the handover contains | What Sam can do next |
| --- | --- |

### One offer, two outcomes

The organiser has an attendance register and A’s statement about the brake adjustment. Those are two sources with different reach. A can say which future they want and what they attempted. The helper can say what they checked. An attendance tick can establish presence under the register’s rules. On its own it leaves the attempted work, the check and A’s continuing capability open. A’s stated aim gives the organiser reason to add a criterion to the working account while keeping the evidence about attendance.

The selective-causal loop is a way to read the encounter. The organiser, A and the helper can hold different accounts, scopes and clocks. Selection can be shared across them, and some changes, such as a slot expiring, may have no evidenced selector. Asking whether someone is available can itself change the situation when that person chooses to offer help.

| Glyph | Read it in the workshop |
| --- | --- |
| $w_t$, $\Pi_i^t$, $x_i(t)$ | The situation at time $t$, the observation route for participant $i$, and what comes through it. Name the targets, exclusions and custody that could affect the decision. A’s statement, the register and the helper’s observation reach different parts of the situation. |
| $M_i$, $H_i$, $B_i$ | The represented map of constraints and routes, the retained history, and the working belief the person uses or has reported. For the organiser, keep A’s dated report and the old attendance record in $H_i$. Attribute the working account $B_i$ from their words or actions within a stated aperture. Their inner mechanism remains outside this evidence. |
| $U_i$, $Q_i$ | Updating the working account on warranted evidence, and posing a question or candidate action from that account and the usable options. A newly stored report may still await review. A proposed check still needs authority to make the inquiry and the helper’s agreement to do the work. |
| $\sigma_i$, $a_i$ | A person’s or a policy’s contribution to selection, and the resulting action, delay or refusal. The organiser can offer the slot, A can accept or decline it, and the helper chooses what they will undertake. A possibility in the map supplies none of those decisions by itself. |
| $\Phi$, $\Pi_j^{t+1}$, ↩ | The change after the actions, a later observation of it and a return to a recipient who can still respond. Booking a session changes the reservation. A’s attempt and the helper’s check are later events. Return their scoped accounts and any disagreement to A while the next choice remains usable. |

With $o$ the organiser and $h$ the helper, the 0.5 schematic functions can be read as follows.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_i(t)&=\Pi_i^t(w_t), &
B_i(t)&=U_i(B_i(t^-),H_i(t),x_i(t)),\\
q_i(t)&=Q_i(B_i(t),\mathcal A_i(t),\mathcal L_i(t)), &
a_i(t)&=\sigma_i(q_i(t),\text{applicable policy or authority}),\\
w_{t+1}&=\Phi(w_t,a_o(t),a_A(t),a_h(t),\varepsilon_t), &
x_j(t+1)&=\Pi_j^{t+1}(w_{t+1}).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\mathcal A_i$ carries the actions in view and $\mathcal L_i$ the applicable limits or value inputs. $\varepsilon_t$ stands for other influences on the transition. The signature describes the distinctions a reader needs. It supplies neither an executable controller nor a theory of how a participant thinks. An attributed revision of $B_i$ establishes a change in the working account at the stated warrant. Changes to the booking or to the bike require their own outcome evidence.

#### Compare the offers

Let $\rho$ be the demonstration and $\pi$ the practice offer. The model $m$ gives A either 20 or 40 minutes, with long-window probability $p$ conditional on the common evidence $E$ available before the offer. The organiser has one slot and learns which window applies only after making the offer. In this model the outcome can occur at the endpoint of A’s window. For either offer in a given world, the completion deadline is the same $T_A=t_0+\text{A’s available minutes}$.

$$
J_m(s;G,T_A)=\Pr_m^s(G\text{ achieved by }T_A\mid E),
\qquad
D_m(\pi:\rho;G,T_A)=J_m(\pi;G,T_A)-J_m(\rho;G,T_A).
$$

The demonstration fits either window and supplies no unaided checked attempt in the session. Practice fits the long window and yields the checked attempt if A takes it and the agreed check happens, as stipulated in the model. So the absolute prospects for attendance and a checked attempt are $(1,0)$ under $\rho$ and $(p,p)$ under $\pi$. The paired differences are $(p-1,p)$. At $p=1/2$ these give the two results in the [first-decision reading](CANDIDATE.md#one-navigation-pass). [Exact replay](navigator_reasons.py) · [Hope comparison and typed outcomes](../SYNTHESIS.md#2-a-common-mathematical-setting)

If the organiser learns the actual time in advance, they can reconsider the offer using that evidence. If the helper withdraws, the practice plan loses a condition the calculation used. Keep a protective outcome, such as A retaining a usable way home by their deadline, in its own field. Establishing the attempt or the check still leaves its scope, A’s ability to leave and the arrangements at home to be evidenced.

In one continuation A has 40 minutes, chooses to try, finishes the attempt and the helper checks it. The helper’s own observation can support the check claim. The organiser’s copy of the helper’s report has the same origin. If A says somebody had to take over part of the task, keep both accounts and check which step they cover. Carry the disagreement or a warranted correction before reporting what A did unaided. Ask A what they want to do next and keep any unfulfilled part of their aim, the choice to leave and the check’s actual scope in the handover.

#### Let A’s reply change the booking

Suppose the venue and helper agree to leave the same slot changeable. The organiser can ask whether A wants to watch, try the adjustment or leave it today, with a confirmed demonstration available while a switch is possible. In this continuation A would ask to watch with 20 minutes and ask to practise with 40. Keep that assumption and the consenting support fixed across the comparison. Within each plan, A can still decline.

On the booking clock, a change takes one minute and must take effect strictly before minute 10. A reply at minute 8 can change the booking at 9. A reply at minute 9 would reach the effect at 10 and miss the lock. This is separate from the inclusive deadline for the check during the session. If a timely request switches each long-window case to practice, the absolute prospects are $(1,p)$ under the same attendance and check assumptions as before. If the switch misses the lock and the fitting demonstration is taken, the prospects are $(1,0)$. Silence leaves that offer available and A’s choice open. Name who keeps the slot and helper’s time free and what that commitment costs. [Campfire’s worked comparison](https://github.com/markgoodbody-bit/COM/issues/954#issuecomment-7161796035)

##### Which replies arrive in time for practice?

Suppose A has 40 minutes on half the occasions and half of all replies reach the desk in time for a switch. Asking leaves A’s time window unchanged. In this comparison A replies each time and takes the demonstration if a requested switch misses.

| When the timely replies arrive | Chance of a checked attempt with the demonstration held open for a switch | Chance with practice booked from the start |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| All are on 40-minute occasions | 1/2 | 1/2 |
| All are on 20-minute occasions | 0 | 1/2 |

Let $p$ be the chance of a 40-minute window and $q$ the chance of a timely reply across the same occasions. The held offer gives A a checked attempt when both events occur. Their overlap $x$ can range from $\max(0,p+q-1)$ to $\min(p,q)$. Multiplying the two half-rates gives $1/4$ under an independence assumption. A attends under the held offer in both rows. With practice booked from the start, A attends on the half of occasions when there is enough time.

Before asking the helper to keep the slot changeable, find out whether a practice request can reach the desk in time on a 40-minute day. If that remains uncertain, explain the two offers to A while A can still choose. The same organiser belief $B_o$ about A’s aim and the feasibility of today’s offer can fit two different cost records with the same prospects. Suppose the practice runs and needs 20 extra minutes of the helper’s time. Here the burden field names its bearer and units.

## 2. People, relationships and possibilities

Ask whose lives, aims and burdens could change; whose voice is absent; who may contest the framing; and what the account does not yet know. A stated goal has an author, context and history. Record evidence about the options and constraints under which it formed without overriding the person's own aim. Assess a result against the aim and criterion in the commitment it concerns. When someone states a new aim, preserve the earlier commitment and date the new choice. Keep any inference about their aim attributed to the observer. Ask which useful routes each participant can take on their own and which depend on others. Read a recorded choice alongside the options the person could use at the time. [See how that changes the next offer](CANDIDATE.md#a-choice-after-the-options-change). Ask how long the person needs access. [Would one quiet Saturday serve C’s aim, or do they need both?](CANDIDATE.md#does-c-need-one-quiet-saturday-or-both)

Supporters are not only resources for a beneficiary: they can form capabilities and futures of their own, including through cooperation, care, learning and maintenance. A possible route in a map, material access to it, chosen participation and sustainable support are different. One participant's benefit does not establish another's consent or obligation.

When people make something together, preserve their different aims, offers, counterproposals, substitutions and refusals. An attempt can produce different results for each person. A claimed usable result needs a named participant, conditions, evidence and relevant horizon. If an arrangement is meant to continue, record who keeps it usable, what triggers upkeep, who carries the work and cost, how it is renewed and how it ends. Mark any terms still unassigned. A one-off contribution can be worthwhile on its own.

When a contribution ends, follow the routes people can still use. Look for a common person, permission or resource in apparently separate substitutes. [Follow a handover through the workshop](CANDIDATE.md#what-survives-a-handover).

People can use a common object while pursuing different aims. A permitted guide or a spare part can be enough for someone to practise a demonstration. Record the resources and permissions that route needs and whose aim it serves.

Curiosity, care, creative work and play can be worthwhile as people experience them now. They need not be reduced to economic output or a risk score. A newly described possibility is not yet a capability formed in the world. A successful shared session does not transfer another person's equipment, establish consent to the session or a later use, or show that future support will be available. Ask what actually became usable, for whom, and on what evidence.

Learning can change an account, a capability, a relationship or a purpose. Ask separately what someone has understood, what they can do under which conditions and support, and what they now want, with evidence for each. These are not mandatory stages. Completing a task with help may be valuable without establishing independent performance; a changed aim may be worthwhile without fulfilling the earlier one. When an account is hard to hear, examine the communication channel and conditions of participation as well as the speaker. Improving the channel may reveal a contribution that was missed; difficulty interpreting an account does not diminish the participant's standing. Ask whether the person actually wants the proposed change.

Prior access to a person's files is not, by itself, permission to reuse them. A previous session does not establish a new session or an obligation to maintain the result. Identify unassigned maintenance as unassigned, and preserve what the supporter actually says separately from another person's account.

### Does C need one quiet Saturday or both?

C wants a quiet place to practise. The organiser has to choose a venue for the next two Saturdays. The first venue guarantees the first Saturday only. At the other venue, one decision covers both dates. In this example there is an even chance of getting both or getting neither. The organiser must choose before that decision is known. Both venues work for C, and the other arrangements are in place whenever the room is available.

| Booking the organiser makes | C has a usable session on at least one Saturday | C has a usable session on both Saturdays |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Book the first venue for one date | 1 | 0 |
| Request both dates at the other venue | 1/2 | 1/2 |

Both bookings average one usable session. The block booking can leave C with neither date, so its chance of at least one usable session is only one half. Ask C which would serve their purpose while both bookings are still possible. If one session is enough, the first venue can guarantee it. If C needs to start something one week and finish it the next, only the second plan can offer that opportunity, at the stated odds. C can decide whether to attend on each date. ## 3. Offers, duties and power

Distinguish offers, prior agreement, ongoing commitments, externally grounded duties and performed actions. Preserve each holder, ground, scope, recipient, trigger, due time, support dependency, renewal, refusal, withdrawal and usable exit where material. Before inviting reliance on a firm offer, check that essential support is agreed and can be delivered in time. Record who undertakes the commitment, their authority where it matters, and what the offer reasonably supports. If support changes, identify who can reach the people relying on it while they still have a usable choice and who can address losses caused by that reliance. Acceptance under pressure is not automatically free agreement. A capacity shortage does not itself erase a duty or settle blame. A choice between competing acceptable plans needs an accountable holder, affected interests and contest route; an equation does not supply legitimacy.

When a decision proceeds with material uncertainties, preserve the decision holder, selected action, reasons and basis, live alternatives, unresolved or disputed claims, potentially foreclosed paths, expected burdens and what still needs review. Proceeding does not itself resolve the uncertainty. This record can support a bounded attempt without turning the account into permission to act.

If a volunteer cannot continue, ask whether another person or institution has a separately grounded responsibility to the affected participant. Name its source, holder, beneficiary and scope when known; an omitted duty-holder is not proof that no duty exists. Do not transfer an unestablished duty to the volunteer or infer a legal answer from the map.

## 4. Change, interaction and evidence of influence

Where a bounded engineered dependency is warranted, name component relations and conditional tests; keep proposed connections distinct from confirmed ones. Do not make association or an editable graph a proof of causal attribution. Where relationships co-evolve and the model cannot support a decomposed causal path, describe interacting constraints and dispositions, diverse plausible explanations, observation and small revisable interventions; do not force additive contribution weights or invented probabilities. Both modes support action, not a claim that the situation has been classified correctly forever. [Who had a way to book the workshop?](CANDIDATE.md#who-had-a-way-to-book)

A stated requirement is a necessary condition within its declared route model, not automatically a sufficient recipe for acting in the world. Nor does one represented route establish that all alternatives have been found. A question proposed by a model does not itself authorise the probe: consider its scope, cost, timing, authority and the participants' constraints. An observation about a participant does not substitute for that participant's own choice.

When a later account differs, check subject, scope and time before calling the earlier one mistaken: the evidence or the world may have changed. Distinguish a proposed revision of an attributed belief, a revision of the evidence account and a claim that the world changed. Record the basis and whether each revision is proposed, accepted under a named contract, rejected or unresolved. Acceptance under a contract does not by itself prove the world changed.

Keep divergent testimony and assess its applicability, source access, corroboration and the costs or beneficiaries of delay under the actual decision contract. A contrary assertion is not automatically decisive, and a witness count does not prove independence. A disputed claim does not by itself choose action or inaction: a separately supported alternative or bounded authorised attempt may remain available. If a decision proceeds, retain the conflict rather than treating it as resolved.

Every search or review selects a target set. Where relevant record who selected it, the basis, known omissions, alternatives and uncertainties. Exclusion from the search is not evidence that a category is absent from the world.

For each intervention, name who may act, what may change, affected scopes, reversibility, indicators, observation limits and a return or stop route. Distinguish feasibility, permission, choice, actuation and actual effect. Unknown evidence can be controlled: identify who authors, controls or can pause the clock, who controls observation timing, who benefits from delay and who bears its costs. UNKNOWN is not automatically neutral or bad faith.

In the stipulated early-versus-late disclosure example in the accompanying simulator, the carrier controls reveal timing. If that choice is the only changed condition, it changes the recipient's best modelled outcome from 7/8 to 3/4, a difference of 1/8. This is attributable to the specified reveal-timing choice in the toy model, not proof of an actual person's motivation or real-world effect. If supported evidence also shows the carrier benefits from delay while the recipient bears its costs, record that possible contamination and its source qualification; control of a clock alone is not enough.

To choose a check, name the claim it would test, what it adds to the sources already in hand, and what someone could do after each answer. Include the time to understand the answer, choose and put the move in effect. Carry who would do the checking and what would be displaced by the wait. [Compare four checks at the workshop](CANDIDATE.md#which-check-can-change-the-booking).

**A practical record for evolving constraints.** Record the particular constraint and its source, who can change or contest it, its role in enabling or limiting different participants, estimated effort and time to change with uncertainty, the relevant planning horizon, potential high-impact changes and what can be observed. Some constraints may support a repeatable activity; others may not. Mark an assessment as disputed or unknown where appropriate. This draws on [Snowden's estuarine mapping, outline steps 1–7](https://thecynefin.co/estuarine-mapping/), which places constraints by change effort and time and suggests monitored portfolios of small interventions. TRACE's addition here is a proposed linkage to affected participants, authority, usable contest and correction; equivalence or added benefit has not been demonstrated. A small trial is not automatically safe for the person bearing its cost.

[Juarrero, *Context Changes Everything*, chapter 6](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/14630.003.0009), distinguishes relations that bring a coordinated pattern into being from the relations through which it persists and shapes what participants can do next. In the workshop, a quiet slot and agreed help let people try the work together. A regular session depends on people keeping arrangements for rooms, opening and equipment in working order. Once established, that routine shapes who can plan around a later session. Check which arrangements the people involved can maintain before inviting continuing reliance. Keep the costs and the possibility of revising or leaving the arrangement visible.

Following [Gordon's ecological questions](https://doi.org/10.1111/ede.12439), examine how an arrangement responds when its conditions change. Who notices, who can alter it, and whose burden makes the response possible? A session that still happens may now depend on extra work; a session that pauses may preserve a usable later opportunity. Compare changing support with changing outcomes. Keep an individual's learning, the group's available arrangement and its capacity to reorganise distinct. These are questions for inquiry, not a score or a presumption that activity should continue. The participant, burden and voluntary-continuation questions are this account's application; they are not established by the biological examples alone.

## 5. Clocks, correction and construction

Track the relevant event ordering. A review after a route closes may improve the record without restoring that route. Protective effect before hardening is strict where specified; a separate delivery-by-deadline contract may be inclusive. A correction route requires accessible capacity and time, not merely a published address. Preserve unattempted alternatives and residual burden when shared capacity cannot satisfy all obligations. For a scheduled opportunity, check what enables each participant to use it in the time available. For a proposed pause, trace the warning to someone who can act within scope, then ask how they can verify the effect before the boundary. The [dispatch case](CANDIDATE.md#a-warning-before-dispatch) works through this route. When an offer keeps a decision open, verify the hold and the route for each answer the offer promises. See the [ranking hold](CANDIDATE.md#hold-the-listing-while-the-musician-decides) and [the musician’s next-season application](CANDIDATE.md#can-the-musician-apply-next-season).

Several organisations responding within their own remit do not necessarily provide a route to the result. Where a route crosses boundaries, identify who coordinates it, who can authorise the necessary steps, what actually reaches the affected target, and who bears navigation and evidence work. A claim moving between offices is not itself a repair.

For a particular harm that hardens at $h$, a protective effect at $e$ must meet $e<h$ to prevent it under that strict boundary. A due-by agreement may instead allow equality. Work backwards from every deadline on the proposed route, including intermediate locks. If two jobs require the same person at the same time, first establish which job they can take and when. Separate timing calculations for the two jobs cannot allocate that person. The [guide booking](README.md#how-late-can-the-answer-arrive) gives an executable example with one named clock and a witness for the limiting deadline.

Urgency, hardening and irreversibility are distinct claims: identify and support the particular boundary being used. Hardening can contribute to an irreversibility claim without establishing it by label alone.

Clock values may be ordered together only under a declared comparable clock or a supported conversion. The laboratory's integer clock is invented; it does not align real sources. A reported failure followed by a later receipt establishes neither continued failure nor restored access or permission.

Compare declared alternatives using the same stated affected scope, aim, protective measures, burden units, baseline, horizon and assumptions; disclose frame changes. A model-relative forecast is not a guaranteed world outcome. A promise is not delivery; delivering one component of a joint effort can cost a contributor even if the other component never arrives. Rest, companionship or maintenance need not show an increase in a selected score to matter.

An event may be unchangeable while its effects can still change. Assess continuing impairment separately from the historical fact, just as a later benefit alone does not establish that a particular earlier loss was repaired.

### Compatibility floor: explicit review meanings

Activate the following when their loss could affect the use; unresolved materiality stays unresolved. These are reading obligations, not claims that the laboratory enforces them:

1. **Selection:** preserve who selected the target, why, known omissions, alternatives and relevant custody. Unsearched is not absent.
2. **Currentness.** Trace the conclusion to the evidence, authority, burdens and routes it depends on. Check which of those changed and leave currentness open when their relevance is unclear. Carry changes in capability, relationships, burdens, routes, clocks and who came into view when they affect the next use. Age alone cannot settle it. In [Sam’s handover](#which-table-did-sams-helper-check), the helper’s departure leaves a completed check of the current table intact. If the helper checked last week’s version, the new one still needs checking.
3. **Warrant at use:** retain a consequential proposition's source, scope, applicability and counterevidence whether it appears as prose, a label, configuration, cache or model output. Copying is not independent verification.
4. **Witness and access:** evidence, access, control and disclosure are different. A specified check is not an executed one; execution may miss the defect or arrive too late. Loss of a witness, heartbeat, reply, route or status may end the current verification interval without establishing why. A different name or an external position alone does not establish witness independence: custody, incentives, derivation or control may still be shared.
5. **Affected scale:** boundaries are selected for a purpose and do not establish personhood or equivalent kinds of entities. Aggregate recovery does not establish that a particular smaller entity or loss was repaired.
6. **Control and exit:** distinguish action, delay, null input and effect; refusal and failure; a formal offer and usable exit. An authority record needs its source, holder, action, scope, limits and applicability. A changed strategy does not undo an executed transition.
7. **Correction-window claim:** name the effect, common clock basis, hardening boundary, supported executable route and alternatives. Strong OPEN needs a supported route before the boundary; unknown alternatives alone do not defeat a supported sufficient route unless they change its necessity, feasibility or claim scope. Strong CLOSED is scoped to the represented aperture and needs evidence the required route cannot complete and no represented alternative removes the requirement. Material uncertainty remains WINDOW_STATUS_UNKNOWN. Use compatible conservative bounds where relevant; scheduling shared resources needs appropriate domain methods.
8. **Burden and residue:** retain type and bearer, and expose any conversion of unlike measures. A later benefit or corrected record does not itself repair a particular earlier loss.
9. **Future correspondence:** name scope, horizon, baseline and value or measure. A reused route label does not prove the same continuation remains reachable; ability to discover and use a route in time differs from its physical operation. More options are not automatically better.
10. **Absence and recurrence:** distinguish UNKNOWN, OMITTED_FROM_RECORD and evidenced ABSENT relative to an expectation. Repeated outcomes can suggest, but do not establish, a shared mechanism. One repair does not prove the mechanism has changed.
11. **Self-application:** examine what this account, its categories, examples and tests select or leave out. It cannot certify itself or the world.
12. **Receiver protection:** use is voluntary. Neither a map nor complexity establishes experience, rights or status. Do not demand secrets, inaccessible internal reasoning, self-modification or unauthorised action as proof of compliance.
13. **Internal mechanisms:** external causal descriptions are hypotheses about internals unless supported by an independent empirical connection; they are not direct observations of a model's internal features.
14. **Stop or handoff:** preserve and route unresolved work to an appropriate established domain owner when further differentiation cannot affect a material claim before its clock, evidence is unavailable, resource or depth limits are reached, or authority belongs elsewhere. Consider the cost of inquiry and the correction window; UNKNOWN alone dictates neither action nor delay.
15. **Practical advantage:** predeclare a use class, relevant ordinary or expert comparator, consequence or transfer criterion, activation-burden measure and ceiling, and adjudication. Where feasible use a blind assessor. A failed matched comparison narrows that use claim; it does not reject the whole purpose. Track maintenance cost when material.

The [17 November comparison](FLOOR-RECONCILIATION.md) records the author’s reading of all fifteen clauses against released 0.5. The [source guide](EDITION-SOURCES.md#the-compatibility-floor) points to the clauses that govern a stronger technical claim. For a decision that turns on a clause, follow its evidence back to the [0.5 floor](TRACE-v0.5-reference.md#inherited-v04-compatibility-floor-review-level-semantics) and the [0.4 source](https://github.com/markgoodbody-bit/TRACE/blob/475772354a29cdd96b3c8fae7be93449c4737d71/TRACE-SPINE.md).

## 8. Sources and limits

The [mathematical companion](README.md) gives the models, inputs, code and expected results for the worked calculations. The [mathematics of supported prospects](../SYNTHESIS.md#6-hope-includes-creating-maintaining-and-recovering-support) distinguishes what keeping support in place makes possible today from how a forecast has changed since yesterday. A model can answer the question it represents. A participant still decides which future they value.

Use the [source guide](EDITION-SOURCES.md) when a decision turns on a distinction the short reading compresses. For example, a report of a broken access route at one instant leaves later access open. A report of receipt under a stated contract establishes only the status that contract defines. The [original restoration record](../../../TRACE-v0.5.md#4-synthetic-encounter-b-a-record-survives-while-a-route-may-not) sets out the events separately. The guide also preserves the original notation, technical profiles and historical cases at their own versions.

The worked encounters and finite tests show how the declared inputs affect a decision. Whether TRACE helps people or systems make better decisions in practice is an empirical question. A fair comparison includes the time it takes to read, use, challenge and maintain the account. The affected people can contest which aims count and how their costs are weighed. Claims about standing, valid consent, law and authority need their own grounds. The [original voluntary invitation](../../../TRACE-v0.5.md#e6-invitation--recover-construct-criticise-or-decline) and the project’s [version-specific training permission](../../../AI_TRAINING_PERMISSION.md) give the terms relevant to those uses.
