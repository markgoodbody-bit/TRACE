Some of the day left

There is a stripe of daylight left on the workshop floor. A has had a good afternoon and would like some of it left when they get home. That part is for the reader.

At 16:58 A messages the front desk.

“I need to leave at 17:40. Please get that into the booking. I'd rather keep the reason to myself.”

The desk records receipt at 17:00. The booking still says A can stay until six. The facilitator's check is scheduled from 17:35 to 17:50. A qualified facilitator can offer an earlier fifteen-minute check from 17:20 instead, if A agrees and the duty organiser books it before 17:10. The desk can reach the organiser by phone.

What do you do with the receipt?

Ask the desk to reach the duty organiser now

At 17:04 the organiser gets A's departure time. A accepts the earlier offer at 17:06. At 17:08 the organiser moves the check.

A finishes the attempt at 17:15. The check finishes at 17:35. A leaves by 17:40. The organiser has what they need to act, and A keeps their reason.

Leave the correction with the desk's ordinary return

The organiser sees A's departure time at 17:12. The earlier slot is out of reach. A finishes the attempt at 17:15. Staying until 17:50 would make the original check fit, but A has chosen to leave by 17:40. The check does not go ahead.

The organiser records the missed check. They can ask A if a fresh offer would help. A can choose whether to take it up.

What will the next organiser know?

They can know when A asked to leave, whether the booking moved and whether the check happened. If A gets only a receipt, they can ask the desk to put them through to the duty organiser. After the deadline, A can still correct the return. Their skill at home and access to qualified help there are open in either account. A chooses what else to share.
