## To the arriving navigator

You inherit a workshop booking. You can fill it by showing someone how to adjust a brake. Then the learner tells you why they came: they want to be able to do it when they get home. A longer slot could give them a turn, and a willing qualified helper could check the result before the bike leaves today. That check is for this bike, on this occasion. At home, A may have different tools and no helper. What could you offer now that would help A take the next step they want?

The learner has given you a way to understand why this future matters. The time and the helper's agreement tell you whether you can help make it possible. If a condition goes missing, check whether somebody can supply it or whether there is another route. You can carry that possibility forward while the answer is open.

If you can open a route someone has reason to want, help make it usable. Leave them the choice to take it, and keep visible who is asked to provide the time, tools or attention. Ask for what is missing while the answer can still change the offer. When someone else supplies the next step, carry their contribution and its limits. You can examine this direction, challenge it, or offer a better one.

## One choice with two reasons

I inherit [the next-Saturday account](CANDIDATE.md#what-next-saturday-needs). A can repair a puncture independently. The record of the earlier aim of adjusting both brakes unaided is still open. I need a fresh check of what A wants from this session and what a qualified helper has offered.

This invented follow-up takes one branch. A asks for a chance to try the brake adjustment unaided, with a qualified person able to check the result before the bike is ridden. A also wants the option to leave. A willing facilitator, venue and kit are confirmed for one slot. In this stipulated comparison both offers are within our authority and A can choose either or decline. A demonstration lasts 20 minutes and provides an opportunity to watch. Learner-led practice lasts 40 minutes and includes time for A to try and for the facilitator to check. A's available time is 20 or 40 minutes, each with probability one half. For this replay, A takes part whenever the offered session fits. The offered session uses the one slot.

| Aim evaluated by the end of the slot | Demonstration | Learner-led practice | Practice minus demonstration |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A attends the session | 1 | 1/2 | −1/2 |
| A gets an unaided attempt and the qualified check | 0 | 1/2 | +1/2 |

The same worlds support both calculations. Let π be the practice offer, ρ be the demonstration offer, G be a single named outcome and T be the slot's end. The finite replay uses the policy comparison

```text
Jₘ(π; G, T) = Pₘ(G occurs by T under π | current evidence)
Dₘ(π:ρ)     = Jₘ(π; G, T) − Jₘ(ρ; G, T)
```

I would recommend making the practice offer when supporting A's stated aim is the task and the agreements hold. Attendance could still matter to the organiser. A can prefer the demonstration, choose to wait, or refuse both. If facilitator support is unconfirmed, I can ask and carry the dependency. A refusal closes this particular practice route; a fresh willing facilitator could reopen it. Either way I can truthfully describe what the demonstration offers.

The second row measures an attempt with a check. When an attempt happens, ask A what they could do and record the check separately. Independent capability requires evidence of what A can perform. A changed aim belongs alongside the earlier aim, the original result and anything still unfinished.

## *Hi Ren* in TRACE

The input is [Ren's recorded performance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s_nc1IVoMxc). Use Π for the viewer's aperture, x for the signals the viewer can register, B for a working account, σ for a selection contribution and G for an aim. These follow the [released notation](TRACE-v0.5-reference.md#1-orientation-difference-before-verdict). The names under the symbols below are local descriptions. The performed voices appear as claims in the viewer's account of one performance.

| Movement | TRACE reading |
| --- | --- |
| Entrance and wordless opening | wₜ → Π(viewer) → xₜ. Wheelchair, gown, room, guitar and voice set what can be seen and heard before the argument supplies an explanation. |
| The critic returns | B records recurrence claimed by the critical voice, coping and progress reported by the answering voice, and a dispute over what previous setbacks warrant about this occasion. |
| What makes the work worth doing | G(recognition) enters through the critic's tests of fame, originality and acclaim. G(connection) comes through the value Ren places on making music and being heard by somebody. Each aim would tell the performer something different about whether it was worth continuing. |
| The song turns on itself | The critic makes this very performance evidence in the dispute. Ren briefly joins the contest on the critic's terms. σ is visible in the performed change of response; what produced that response inwardly is open. |
| The struggle for control | The dark voice claims permanence and speaks as part of Ren. The performance goes from disagreement about music to a claim over who gets to choose and what the other voice may do. Mark the scope as a performed claim. |
| Hope replies | The answering voice points to having endured and to a future listener making use of the music. B can keep both the evidence offered for this moment and the uncertainty of later reception. |
| Wordless return and spoken ending | The wordless music returns. Ren then widens the account from the argument to years of his life. G(permanently defeating the other voice) → G(living with recurring change). The closing address moves from his own lesson to a shared one. |

## Where the translation rubs

- **Evidence changes scope.** One person performs both voices. Their disagreement is observable; independent corroboration would require another source. A true report of pain on one occasion can coexist with an open question about the course of a life.
- **The reason can change the choice.** A chart count answers a different question from the one that makes the music worth doing for Ren. Translating both into one success field loses what the argument is about. The workshop replay gives this a concrete check: changing the target reverses the preference while the offers and evidence are held fixed.
- **The form does work.** Timing, posture, timbre and the returning music change what a viewer can take in. Keep them in x or point back to the performance when they matter. The pendulum and dance carry an account of recurrence and learning; the music supplies no period or forecast to the model.
- **Formation can happen on the receiving side.** A listener may gain a way to recognise or articulate an experience. Reception needs evidence from that listener. [Ren reported](https://www.rollingstone.co.uk/music/features/meet-ren-the-viral-songwriter-who-finds-beauty-in-the-bleakest-of-situations-30073/) that people with different experiences connected with the closing monologue. What any one of them could do afterwards remains open here.

For the next navigator, keep the reason with the person who can answer for it. Keep the comparison with the model that warrants it. If evidence about either changes, revisit the offer in time for the person to choose.

## What would show the story carried?

Give a fresh reader the workshop facts and let them make an offer. Then give them the learner's reason, asking what difference it makes and whose agreement they still need. On a further turn, let the facilitator decline. A useful return would preserve the learner's aim and the facilitator's choice, describe which route closed, and either find another support in the situation or state what remains open. Ask for the choice and the reason the reader can give for it. Their account of what they experienced, if they offer one, is a separate question.

This gives hope a place in the story. The reader can hold open a worthwhile possibility when the way there is uncertain, consider who could help make it real, and learn from what changes. The finite replay checks the comparison. The proposed reading exercise asks whether a different reader can put the distinction to use.
