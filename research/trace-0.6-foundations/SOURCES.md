## Amartya Sen — Equality of What? (1979 lecture; 1980 publication)

Primary text: https://tannerlectures.org/documents/sen80/

Official lecture record: https://tannerlectures.org/lectures/equality-of-what/

Read the official text, particularly sections 3–5. Sen argues that holdings and utility can miss what a person can actually do, because conversion from resources differs between people. He also recognises difficulties in indexing capabilities and treats this as a partial moral guide. This supports keeping supplied resources, effective opportunities and subjective response distinct. It does not establish our probability model, a total ordering of lives, or an exhaustive ethics.

## Munindar P. Singh — Semantical Considerations on Dialectical and Practical Commitments (AAAI 2008)

Author-hosted paper: https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/mpsingh/papers/mas/aaai-08-commitments.pdf

Proceedings record: https://www.aiinternational.org/Library/AAAI/2008/aaai08-028.php

Read the introduction, formal model and postulates. Singh distinguishes assertions from undertakings to bring about conditions, models conditional commitments, and shows that apparently natural postulates depend on temporal and semantic constraints. His social commitments concern one agent to another, rather than self-commitment. This supports typed, time-sensitive records and cautions against inferring delivery from assertion. Our expanded fields, normative validity checks, resource accounting and self-undertaking category are additional proposals, not Singh's established results.

## Michael E. Bratman — A Planning Theory of Acting Together (2022)

Open primary article: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-american-philosophical-association/article/planning-theory-of-acting-together/36733ED653A15D9E3F113F7B9FFF4E64

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/apa.2021.17

Read the article's core argument. Bratman links temporally organised planning to shared activity through interlocking intentions, compatible subplans, mutual responsiveness and persistence interdependence. He distinguishes this explanatory structure from interpersonal moral obligations, whose grounds require additional normative defence; coercion, deception and immoral shared activity expose the issue. This supports separating successful coordination from legitimate obligation. The paper concerns human planning agency; it does not establish AI experience, our proposed moral compass, or the validity of every promise.
