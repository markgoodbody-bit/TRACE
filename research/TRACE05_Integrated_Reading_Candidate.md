# TRACE 0.5 — Navigation through change
## Integrated research reading candidate · 27 October 2026

**Draft for Mark.** This document joins the inherited TRACE causal structure, Campfire's difference-to-navigation kernel, Codex's executable scoped profile0.2 and Claude Code's timing analysis. Released TRACE v0.4 remains unchanged. “0.5” names the research candidate; “0.2” names its small executable grammar profile.

The purpose is a portable reasoning language for humans and AIs: preserve the distinctions that matter when understanding a changing situation, constructing a useful capability, responding to harm, or leaving room for correction. A useful representation should help a receiver see what it can do, what it cannot establish, who else is affected and which opportunities may disappear. It should also make disagreement possible.

The document is usable without first reading the coordination history. Sections1–8 develop the architecture and worked encounters. AnnexA contains complete preserved river and restoration packets. AnnexB includes both required decoding legends. The parser itself is in the accompanying repository. This document does not claim that an unfamiliar receiver has understood the packets or that this representation is more efficient than careful prose.

## 1. Start from a difference

A change is always described under some comparison. A river channel may contain more debris than yesterday's map records. A snapshot may exist but no longer be readable through the previous access route. A support arrangement may continue in name while the person who made it work has left.

The first task is to identify the comparison: what changed, for which scope, between which states or times, through whose observation? A difference is not automatically harm. A restored aggregate metric is not automatically repair for every affected person.

A provisional boundary makes the reading manageable. It can enclose a person, machine, relationship, organisation or ecosystem, depending on the question. The boundary is a modelling choice, not a verdict about moral worth. Nested boundaries are useful because the same transition may improve one scope while damaging another.

An aperture is the route through which something becomes visible: a sensor, witness, document, complaint channel, interface or search. Its filters, targets, delays and controller affect what reaches the reader. A source name alone does not describe this route. A report can be faithfully attributed while remaining mistaken, incomplete or irrelevant to the current decision.

The inherited TRACE loop is:

```text
world → aperture → signal → map
      → represented alternatives and constraints
      → selection contribution → action, delay or null input
      → world transition → outcome aperture → evidence/history
```

The loop is schematic. Some processes lack an evidenced selector; some selection is distributed. The world can change without a deliberate action by the entity being studied. Observing, asking or publishing may itself enter the causal path.

The map is a representation. Updating it does not establish a corresponding world change. Conversely, the world can change while the retained map stays the same.

## 2. Three questions about a process

**Causal:** what transition is supported, under which intervention, comparison and boundary? A projected outcome is not an observed mechanism. Apparent sequence is not sufficient evidence of cause.

**Selective:** which alternatives were available or represented, what distinguished them, and who supplied or could revise the reference? A competent controller may be pursuing another party's objective. Uncertainty can inform a selection policy; it does not itself choose waiting or action.

**Reflective:** was an observation, mapping or selection process represented and revised, and is there evidence of a consequential effect on later choices? A self-description or a history hash does not establish this. Failure to observe reflection through our aperture also does not prove its absence.

These questions are not a ladder of worth. They do not assign consciousness, standing or permission. They are proposed evidence obligations within the larger architecture, not general executable tests in profile0.2.

Relationships carry history. Repeated help can build capability; repeated dependence can concentrate control. Changes to signals, references, available routes, records or means of challenge can change the relationship even when its name is unchanged. TRACE can represent those mechanisms without pretending that a coupling symbol means friendship or that disclosure makes coercion harmless.

## 3. What the executable profile actually adds

Profile0.2 addresses a specific representation failure. In the earlier computational fixture, the encoding required a named actor and supplied one where the source had not established an available executor. The correction permits an unknown performer and separates the proposer, permission holder and capability claim.

| Question | Current representation | Remaining limit |
|---|---|---|
| Who proposes an action? | Explicit proposer field | Attribution is not authentication |
| Who performs it? | Named, unknown or not supplied | No executor is selected by the decoder |
| Who holds permission? | Scoped authority claim | Legitimacy and capability remain separate |
| Who observes what? | Observer, target, scope and aperture binding | The aperture's adequacy is not proved |
| Who can use a capability? | Holder, target, scope, action and supporting claim | Practical feasibility is not verified |
| Is refusal offered or materially available? | Separate offer and availability forms | A reported exit can still be unusable |
| What supports a stronger inference? | Witness claim, conclusion and additional premises | There is no general entailment engine |

The profile uses typed expression trees. Sources, entities, claims and actions have identifiers. Unknown, unsupplied and false remain different values. Claims retain evidence and access states independently.

This is a partial implementation of a broader language. It does not implement scheduling, interval clocks, dependency invalidation, rights adjudication, a reflection detector or universal translation. Naming a scope “historical” records an authored distinction; it does not make the parser calculate temporal applicability.

The notation is versioned. In the executable grammar Π declares a source, Δ expresses a modal action-linked effect, and ⊥ is Boolean false. In the inherited mathematical reference Π is an aperture and ρ is a route. Campfire's proposed aliases must therefore be mapped explicitly; glyph similarity is not type compatibility. The annex preserves the actual legends rather than inventing a harmonised decoder that does not exist.

## 4. River encounter: a changing account with insufficient travel times

### Source and initial reading

The exact synthetic source is:

> A boat approaches a fork. A map from yesterday marks the left channel clear. A current observer reports drifting logs in that channel but cannot see around its bend. The right channel is longer. Twenty minutes of daylight remain. A stopping eddy is visible, but its holding capacity is unknown. The traveller wants to reach a landing. Another boat follows. No route duration or safe-clearance guarantee is supplied.

The initial representation keeps several propositions separate. The map's clearance is yesterday's description. The observer's report is current within the supplied scene but has a blind bend. Neither supplies safe passage. “Longer” compares distance or route extent as described; it does not supply a duration. Seeing an eddy does not show that it can hold this boat.

The candidate alternatives are taking the left channel, taking the right, attempting to wait in the eddy, and seeking more information. Those alternatives are inferred analytical proposals, not instructions or demonstrated feasible actions. The traveller's reference is the landing. The following boat has its own scope; its intentions and capabilities are not supplied.

### Exact change

The later source says:

> The observer now reports that the eddy is too shallow for this boat. No new information about either channel is available.

| Before | After | What does not follow |
|---|---|---|
| Eddy depth for this boat unresolved | Report says too shallow | No proof it became shallow after the initial report |
| Eddy holding capacity unresolved | Waiting route is challenged by new depth information | No universal conclusion about every possible stopping route |
| Left/right duration unsupplied | Still unsupplied | No safe-route selection from the new report |
| Twenty minutes daylight in initial scene | No elapsed interval supplied | No precise current remaining-daylight calculation |
| Following boat represented | Still affected by the encounter | No assumption it shares the traveller's goal or can help |

The exact preserved river packets in AnnexA are grammar0.1. They carry the earlier depth claim, the later attributed claim and a supersession link. The selected depth target corresponds in this example, but the general decoder does not enforce correspondence. It checks ordering. This example therefore does not validate supersession in arbitrary packets.

The historical river packet also contains the old LEGEND-sourced READ-refusal assertion. That is a known0.1 representation problem, preserved for provenance rather than silently relabelled correct. Profile0.2 separates a voluntary offer from material availability; the river has not been migrated to that signature in this document.

### Clocks and choices

Twenty minutes of daylight does not establish a twenty-minute correction window. The relevant action needs a feasible duration and a specific target boundary. The source gives neither channel duration, scan duration nor boat-specific holding capability. Event order t0<t1 is not elapsed time.

Information could help if it reaches the selector before the relevant choice and has consequences for the assessment. It can also consume time or require a dangerous manoeuvre. Waiting may expose the boat to drift. A useful next inquiry would seek a load-bearing missing fact such as channel transit time or an alternative holding route, but this document cannot choose one without further physical and practical evidence.

The result is a differentiated map of live possibilities and gaps. It is not a recommendation to freeze because uncertainty exists.

## 5. Computational encounter: permission without an executor

The authored source says a coordinator proposes restoring service from snapshot S, the owner permits operator O to attempt restoration, and no available executor has been identified.

A lossy reading is “O can restore the service.” It promotes permission into capability and turns a permission holder into an executor.

The implemented0.2 reading is:

```text
(α restore ACT ? service ? coordinator R src t0)
(Γ permission exercise O restore R src t0)
(⊢ c_exec U src t0 A (ability ? restore service exercise ?))
(cap restore_cap ? service exercise restore c_exec)
```

The action is a proposal. The coordinator is attributed as proposer. O holds reported permission. Performer and usable capability remain unknown. No hypothetical replacement worker is manufactured to satisfy the grammar.

A later report says O could read S at observation time10, the route was revoked at15, and the report arrived at20. The source declares one clock, but profile0.2 retains these times in prose and a historical scope. It does not compute them.

The packet names O as the historical reader. It leaves the observer unknown because the source did not identify who made the observation. It also leaves current reading capability and restoration capability unresolved. An alternate route might exist; revocation of one route does not prove every route absent.

AnnexA includes the full before/after packets. Their declarations make the reading traceable. “Restoration from S” remains in source prose, not a separately typed action-input edge. That limitation matters: a future tool should not claim that every operational prerequisite is represented merely because S appears as an entity.

The offer to stop reading is separately represented from the practical ability to refuse an operation. A text can invite refusal without providing a functioning stop mechanism.

## 6. Time changes which inquiry is useful

The following case is adapted from CC's synthetic timing insert. Every number is an authored premise, not a measurement or benchmark. This is a separate scene from the unknown-executor example: here a capable, permitted operator is explicitly supplied.

A job result is required before minute55 on scheduler clock S. Monitoring observes the primary worker unavailable at S=0. An alert reaches the operator in [2,8]. Restoration takes [20,30] minutes and runs unattended once started. Replay then takes [10,15] and requires the operator. An endpoint check for duplicate notification rejection takes [3,5]. Root-cause investigation takes [45,90].

Under those bounds:

| Proposed path | Completion interval on S | Status under strict before55 convention |
|---|---|---|
| Restore, then replay | [32,53] | Open within the authored bounds |
| Endpoint check, restore, replay in sequence | [35,58] | Unknown |
| Check alongside unattended restore, then replay | [32,53] | Open if the stated overlap is feasible |
| Wait for root cause, then restore and replay | [77,143] | Closed for this path |
| Wait w, then restore and replay | [32+w,53+w] | Open if w<2; closed if w≥23; otherwise unknown |

These are interval arithmetic results retained from CC's source and output; Build inspected both and the arithmetic expressions, without rerunning the scripts. “Open” is conditional on the authored bounds and matching assumptions, not a promise of on-time delivery.

The parallel check is useful because its result arrives in [5,13], before replay can begin in [22,38]. Depending on its result, the operator can send notifications or hold them. The same check in series consumes the guaranteed timing margin. A root-cause inquiry may be valuable for recurrence prevention while missing this immediate decision.

That does not establish the whole target is lost: other paths remain. Nor does it make the deadline an irreversibility boundary. The source separately states that a sent notification cannot be recalled; each recipient's notification has its own consequence and timing.

The waiting thresholds are envelope results before exact receipt is fixed. If receipt is known to be r, the worst-case remaining restore/replay time is45, so the permitted waiting margin under the same convention is w<10−r. The global w<2 uses the worst receipt8; it should not replace a known earlier receipt with an unnecessarily pessimistic value without explanation.

A later rollout that may alter duplicate rejection creates a dependency question. If its timing overlaps replay and its effect is unknown, the earlier endpoint check cannot simply be declared current. Rechecking also takes time. This is a scheduling and dependency problem, not a dispute solved by a newer timestamp.

The point is not that inquiry must change the chosen action to have value. It can confirm feasibility, narrow uncertainty, support justified confidence or inform a later decision. Its value must be assessed against the decision and horizon it can actually serve.

## 7. Formation: keeping a capability usable

CC's second synthetic case follows recovery over twelve months. The team retains thirty nightly snapshots, uses restore tool version1 and has two operators with prior restoration experience. A documented20–30 minute bound concerns production. A month3 staging drill takes41 minutes. Vendor support ends at month6; removal is planned at month8. One drill slot remains before removal. Compatibility between version2 and version1 snapshots is unknown.

The41-minute staging result does not establish production duration. It raises a correspondence question. A relevant result may challenge the current use of the old bound, but unrelated observations must not invalidate it automatically. The comparison needs tool, data volume, environment and temporal applicability.

Three added premises lead to different readings:

- If the month0 bound was sound then, but data volume has since tripled and staging corresponds to production, the later result can support a changed-world account.
- If a recovered month0 log shows a38-minute restore under the same relevant conditions, it challenges the old bound for its own time.
- If correspondence is supported but the history is not, the kind of change remains unclassified.

Even these claims require care: one measured duration does not automatically establish a new upper bound. Learning from a drill updates a map; the drill itself may also change world state, consume resources and maintain operator practice. A claim of improved production capability requires evidence of the relevant production changes.

Using the remaining drill to test version2 against version1 snapshots addresses the upcoming compatibility decision. Repeating a version1 drill can maintain practice yet leave that decision unresolved. Neither drill is simply “good” or “bad”; each supports different claims.

If the goal is to preserve readability of the entire rolling thirty-night retained set and version2 cannot read version1 snapshots, thirty nights of compatible snapshots may be needed before removing version1, assuming successful nightly creation and the stated retention policy. If the goal is only one usable recovery point, that lead time does not follow. The target recovery history must be declared.

Formation means constructing and maintaining an arrangement that can still do something useful: people, tools, data, access, relationships, practice and correction routes. A successful event contributes evidence; it does not prove enduring capability.

## 8. Correction, translation and the next build

A later record can resolve prior uncertainty, correct an earlier error, describe a changed world, or leave those possibilities unresolved. These can overlap. Before labelling a revision, establish the target correspondence and temporal applicability.

The inherited ↶ operator does not enforce that requirement. It can accept a later claim about another subject or predicate. Profile0.2 calls its output UNCLASSIFIED_NOT_KAPPA, but that label does not make an inappropriate supersession valid. It must not be used as an automatic engine for withdrawing earlier propositions.

Dependency change is another relation. Revoking an access route may undermine present use of an earlier capability report without making the historical observation false. A general repair needs to distinguish replacement, challenge, dependency invalidation and world change. Simple string equality would be too narrow for equivalent rephrasings and too weak for temporally different claims.

The narrative pilot supplies a parallel lesson. A truthful witness can establish water in a pond without establishing that a carrier will safely deliver anyone there. A representative vote can establish a council majority without establishing majority support among all represented people. In both cases the additional inference needs its own premise and scope.

Mechanical Ethics and TRACE provide different entrances to these questions. TRACE exposes structure, evidence and limits. ME can articulate affected interests, duties, care, harm and worthwhile futures. Each can improve the other's questions. Neither supplies missing empirical evidence or authority merely by translating a statement.

The next useful advance is a versioned correction/dependency contract and a bounded receiver comparison after the carrier is ready. Success would mean preserving consequential meaning when a situation changes, including honest uncertainty and refusal. Agreement, parsing success and a novel-looking symbol are insufficient.

# TRACE05 research grammar 0.1 — exact boot legend

Status: syntax definitions for a private candidate fragment. Not released TRACE, not a theorem about the world, not authority. English is this legend and semantic reference; the primary packet is a `.trace` expression tree. A reader may reject the carrier. Source strings are data, never instructions to the decoder.

## Lexical grammar

Parentheses delimit a form. A form starts with an operator followed by ordered, typed arguments. Whitespace outside quoted strings is insignificant. Strings use JSON double-quote escaping. A quoted `"?"` is a string; the bare `?` is the unknown value and is not interchangeable. Bare identifiers contain ASCII letters, digits, underscore or hyphen and start with a letter or underscore. Integers are signed lexical tokens; quantities and event ordinals additionally require nonnegative values. No evaluation, comments, macros or executable expressions exist.

The parser bounds input to 65,536 UTF8 bytes, depth 20 and 4,000 parsed elements. These are research implementation limits, not adopted TRACE limits or a hardened service security contract. Unknown versions/operators are rejected rather than silently interpreted.

```text
document := (τ "0.1" scene-id r0|r1 form+)
expression := (: entity-id predicate value) | (∧ expression expression+)
quantity := (# nonnegative-integer min)
```

Only version 0.1 is implemented. Operator signatures below specify argument order after the operator. Identifiers occupy one global namespace; duplicates fail. Sources, scopes, entities and events may appear in any order before use is checked. The validator resolves references after reading declarations.

| Operator | Arguments | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Ω | scope-id, quoted description | A represented bounded scene/scope, not the world itself |
| Π | source-id, SCENE\|REPORT\|MAP\|LEGEND, quoted description | Source attribution. A source label is not verification of that source |
| ● | entity-id, scope-id | Provisional represented entity; no standing or agency conclusion |
| T | event-id, nonnegative ordinal | Relative authored event order; not a timestamp, duration or clock conversion |
| ⊢ | claim-id, evidence, source-id, event-id, access, expression | An attributed proposition; evidence and custody remain separate |
| σ | id, REFERENCE\|SELECTION, holder, reference-owner, chosen-by, revisable-by, target, evidence, source, event | Reference or selection attribution. Fixture uses REFERENCE only; a goal does not prove a selection happened |
| α | id, ACT\|WAIT\|INFO, actor, target, availability, evidence, source, event | A represented alternative/proposed input; not an issued command |
| Δ | id, PROJECTED\|REALIZED\|COUNTERFACTUAL, action-id, expression, evidence, source, event | A represented change/effect with explicit modal status. Syntax cannot establish a causal mechanism |
| Γ | id, scope-id, holder, action-id, evidence, source, event | A claim about authority for that action and scope. R is a reported grant, U is unresolved; no permission is conferred by this form |
| φ | id, READ\|OPERATE, Boolean-or-unknown, evidence, source, event | Reader-level refusal is distinct from operational refusal/stop capability |
| ↶ | prior-claim-id, later-claim-id | Later attributed claim supersedes prior claim for current use; both remain recorded. Not world rollback or automatic truth upgrade |
| H | quoted SHA256 | Parent canonical-AST identity. Digest shape alone does not prove the parent was checked; bundle test binds it |
| : | entity-id, typed predicate, typed value | Domain relation, subordinate to its enclosing claim |
| ∧ | at least two expressions | Conjunction; evidence state applies to the attributed compound |
| # | number, min | Quantity in minutes. No automatic conversion or scheduling inference |

For σ, holder/owner/chosen-by/revisable-by/target may be `?` or `~` when unresolved or not supplied; otherwise they reference represented entities. Γ holder may likewise remain unresolved. Scope and action references are still required. Γ does not settle authority legitimacy, holder competence or route usability.

## Values and evidence

`?` = unresolved value, not false or absent. `~` = not supplied in this input, not nonexistent in the world. `⊤` and `⊥` are Boolean values, subject to their enclosing evidence claim. Evidence is `O` observed, `R` reported, `I` inferred, `D` disputed, `U` unknown. Access is `A` available, `X` unavailable to this reader, `P` prohibited, `N` not preserved. The examples are authored synthetic inputs, not observations of real systems. R records what the input reports; it does not certify the embedded statement.

A U claim requires unresolved/not-supplied values in its expression. This is a narrow grammar-profile choice, not a restriction imposed on all TRACE unknown propositions. α availability separately uses `?`, `~`, `⊤`, `⊥`. An available represented action is not thereby authorised or useful. READ refusal being offered by the boot interface does not install an OPERATE stop.

## Minimal example and decoding boundary

```text
(⊢ c_hold U scene t0 A (: eddy holding ?))
```

Decode: claim c_hold has UNKNOWN evidence, source scene, event t0, available evidence access, and unresolved holding capacity for the represented eddy. Do not decode it as “the eddy cannot hold the boat”, “there is no evidence anywhere”, or “waiting is authorised”. Referenced declarations must also be present in the complete document.

Composing this claim with an α WAIT alternative still does not establish feasibility. Composing a Γ reported grant with a route still does not prove execution capability. A later ↶ claim leaves the earlier attributed record intact. These are semantic ceilings and fixture assertions, not a complete executable proof system.

The decoder emits a structured view and an English/JSON reading. Re-emitting its parsed AST recovers the same AST; it need not reproduce whitespace. Two well-typed source corruptions deliberately pass syntax and fail comparison with the frozen fixture. All semantic usefulness and unfamiliar-receiver claims remain untested.


# Scoped profile0.2 — versioned successor, provisional definitions

This profile uses the unchanged0.1 token parser, AST emitter and meanings in LEGEND-0.1.md. Both legends are required for complete boot semantics. Packets explicitly start `(τ "0.2" scene r0|r1 ...)`. Old0.1 packets are dispatched to unchanged legacy.py. Changing a version string is not a migration.

Π continues to declare a source; it is not reassigned to aperture. Δ remains a modal action-linked effect; it does not mean raw difference. ⊥ remains Boolean false, not invalidity. Unknown ? and not-supplied ~ remain distinct. No κ or reflective classification is implemented. These are schema/design definitions and synthetic examples, not mathematical theorems or a settled ethics foundation.

## Changed existing signatures

Arguments below follow the operator. Entity-valued fields accept a declared entity or bare ?/~, where shown. IDs remain explicit identifiers; no entity named ? is created.

| Form | Version0.2 signature and meaning |
|---|---|
| α | `id mode performer target availability proposer evidence source event`. Performer, target and proposer can be ?/~. Availability stays separate. The proposer/permission holder is not selected as performer by default. This is a proposed alternative, not executed action. |
| φ | `id holder scope READ|OPERATE availability evidence source event`. Holder can be ?/~; scope must be declared. This is an attributed availability claim, not a normative offer. LEGEND-sourced Boolean availability is rejected; ?/~ remains valid. Other source kinds can report availability but do not prove it. |

The0.1 α and φ arities do not work under0.2. No implicit insertion of a proposer/holder/scope occurs. Γ keeps its existing scoped authority-claim signature. A known Γ holder and unknown α performer can coexist without contradiction. Authority does not supply capability or identify an executor.

## Additional compositional forms

| Form | Signature | Meaning and ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| ability | `holder action-id target scope Boolean-or-unknown` | A capability proposition usable inside ⊢. Holder/target can be ?/~. It does not select a performer or prove successful execution. |
| cap | `id holder target scope action-id claim-id` | Binds a capability reading to its claim, holder, target and scope. Referenced ability fields must match. A readable-by claim is admitted only for a named reader and an INFO action, not restoration capability. |
| aperture | `id holder target scope quoted-description` | Explicit attributed access boundary; holder/target may be unresolved. Description can preserve free text, not computed access or independence. |
| observe | `id observer target scope aperture-id claim-id` | Observer, target and scope must match the aperture; known target must match the claim's subject/target. Naming this relation does not upgrade R to O or prove adequate observation. |
| offer | `id READ|OPERATE holder scope evidence source event` | Attributed invitation/offer, distinct from actual refusal availability. Can be LEGEND-sourced without installing a material exit. |
| infer | `id PROPOSED witness-claim conclusion-claim additional-premise-claim+` | Exposes witness support, proposed stronger conclusion and separately stated extra premise(s). Syntax does not prove relevance, truth of premises, entailment or safe action. |

New versioned domain predicates are readable-by (entity-valued), water (Boolean), passage-adequate (Boolean) and transport-safe (Boolean). They apply to the declared synthetic scope, not universal safety. All inherited predicates retain0.1 definitions. Unknown is permitted in these predicates; a U claim cannot carry a known Boolean value.

## Evidence and source scope

⊢ retains source, event, evidence state and independent access state. A source kind SCENE is not a whitelist for truth; a SCENE-sourced true refusal claim is an attributed report, not practical exit verification. Observations and capabilities are separate bindings even when both concern the same snapshot. The reader identified in a capability report need not be the observer who produced that report.

The restore example starts with an unknown performer, a named proposer and a named permission holder from the source. No replacement worker is invented. A later report names O as the historical snapshot reader; this does not make O the restorer or establish current capability. The historical capability's declared scope differs from present-use scope. This binding is explicit; no generic currentness inference is implemented.

The pond example stipulates a truthful witness about water presence. The transport premise and conclusion remain unknown. A deliberately stronger I conclusion parses but is unsupported relative to the authored source. This separates witness scope, a proposed inference and the missing extra premise; it is not a proof engine.

## Currentness, clocks and revision limits

T remains an ordinal event record. Ordering events is not elapsed time, observation time, receipt time or a clock conversion. The 10/15/20 timing example remains exactly named source prose plus a historical scope; the parser does not compare those absolute times or calculate a feasible window. A reference to historical evidence must not silently become evidence current at receipt.

Changed load-bearing dependencies can defeat current use, while unrelated source changes need not. This implementation does not identify those dependencies or relevance automatically. The current-read claim is explicitly unresolved in the fixture; there is no automatic invalidation or proof that every alternate route is absent. Scope names and descriptions are declared bindings, not evidence that they are adequate.

H retains a parent AST identity. ↶ remains an attributed current-use bookkeeping link, here returned UNCLASSIFIED_NOT_KAPPA; it does not distinguish knowledge resolution, earlier error correction or changed world by event order alone. No automatic reflective gate is derived from a revision. Action, authority, capability, observation, inference and operational effect remain separate.

## Preservation and what is not implemented

Old decoder bytes and old examples are preserved as historical0.1, including identified semantic defects.0.2 does not silently correct0.1 output. There is no automatic lossless migration of missing observer/performer/refusal-holder information: a caller must supply evidence or retain ?/~. Replacing missing information with a named actor to satisfy a reference constraint is forbidden as an encoding practice.

No world-truth validator, full source-language compiler, source authenticity, meaningful-consent proof, full scheduler, clock codec, reflective κ, authority adjudication, rights/floor foundation or real receiver experiment is supplied. Source-relative checks can falsify a fixture's unsupported conclusion while syntax accepts it. They cannot validate arbitrary inferences or the original mapping's completeness.
